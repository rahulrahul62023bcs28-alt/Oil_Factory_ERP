\section{Slide 4: Expected Outcomes --- Structured Notes}

\subsection{1. Smart Factory Management (Operational Digitization)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Problem Solved:} Eliminates error-prone manual physical ledgers, scattered paper weighbridge slips, and offline Excel sheets that cause operational delays and data discrepancies.
    \item \textbf{Centralized Reactive ERP:} Unifies all factory operations into a single responsive web interface:
    \[
    \text{Raw Seed Inward} \longrightarrow \text{Storage Silos} \longrightarrow \text{Expeller Crushing} \longrightarrow \text{Oil Storage Tanks} \longrightarrow \text{Sales Billing} \longrightarrow \text{Workforce}
    \]
    \item \textbf{Real-Time Synchronization (Socket.io):} Emits live WebSocket events across clients. Whenever a production batch or sales dispatch occurs, all dashboard gauges update instantly without requiring a page reload.
    \item \textbf{Role-Based Access Control (RBAC):} Enforces JSON Web Token (JWT) authorization across three distinct privilege tiers: \texttt{Administrator}, \texttt{Factory Manager}, and \texttt{Factory Staff}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{2. Accurate Oil Yield Prediction (AI Machine Learning Engine)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Supervised Regression Engine:} Uses \textbf{Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) Linear Regression} to predict output volume (in Liters) from raw seed input (in Kilograms) \textit{prior} to physical crushing.
    \item \textbf{High Precision:} Achieves a goodness-of-fit of $\mathbf{R^2 > 0.999}$ and a Mean Absolute Error ($\text{MAE}$) below $\mathbf{\pm 2.4\text{ Liters}}$ across commercial batch sizes ($500\text{--}5000\text{ kg}$).
    \item \textbf{Inefficiency \& Wear Detection:} Calculates extraction variance:
    \[
    \Delta = \text{Actual Batch Liters} - \text{ML Predicted Liters}
    \]
    If $\Delta < -5\%$, the system flags machine slippage, pressing temperature loss, or high seed moisture.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{3. Better Inventory and Production Control}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Multi-Tank Telemetry:} Live visual level gauges across 5 oil tanks: Groundnut, Sunflower, Mustard, Sesame, and Coconut.
    \item \textbf{Automated Threshold Safeguards:} Dispatches instant warnings on low stock levels ($< 1000\text{ L}$) and enforces an upper ceiling ($10,000\text{ L}$) to prevent tank overflow.
    \item \textbf{Atomic Stock Mutations:} Adding a completed batch automatically increments storage, while dispatching a sales invoice atomically deducts inventory, preventing race conditions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Slide 5: Dataset Details --- Three-Tier Architecture}

Our project utilizes a structured \textbf{Three-Tier Dataset Architecture} to power machine learning training, ground-truth benchmarking, and transactional ERP operations:

\subsection{Tier 1: Historical Extraction Dataset (Primary ML Dataset --- \texttt{ml/dataset.csv})}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Storage Format:} Local CSV file (\texttt{ml/dataset.csv}).
    \item \textbf{Data Volume:} 40 structured batch extraction records spanning 5 edible oil types.
    \item \textbf{Batch Size Range:} Commercial factory operational range of \textbf{500 kg to 5,000 kg}.
    \item \textbf{Features and Target Variables:}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \texttt{oil\_type} (\textit{Categorical}): Groundnut Oil, Sunflower Oil, Mustard Oil, Sesame Oil, Coconut Oil.
        \item \texttt{raw\_seed\_kg} (\textit{Independent Variable $X$}): Weight of seed batch fed into the expeller (in kg).
        \item \texttt{extracted\_liters} (\textit{Dependent Target $y$}): Volume of clean oil extracted (in Liters).
        \item \texttt{extraction\_efficiency\_pct} (\textit{Verification Metric}): $\frac{\text{extracted\_liters}}{\text{raw\_seed\_kg}} \times 100\%$.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Mathematical Equation (OLS Linear Regression):}
    \[
    \mathbf{y = m \cdot x + c} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{Extracted Liters} = (m \times \text{Seed Weight in Kg}) + c
    \]
    where $m$ is the extraction recovery slope and $c$ is the machine tare absorption constant.
\end{itemize}

\section{Top 10 Viva \& Panel Member Questions and Answers}

\begin{qabox}[Q1: Why did you choose Linear Regression instead of Random Forest or a Deep Neural Network?]
\textbf{Answer:} ``In mechanical seed crushing, oil extraction is governed by the conservation of mass: \textbf{oil yield is intrinsically directly proportional to seed input weight ($V \propto M$)}. Because the physical system has a single continuous independent variable per variety, Linear Regression provides the most accurate mathematical fit ($R^2 > 0.999$). Deep learning or ensemble models on medium batch data would severely overfit, introduce high latency, and lack interpretability. OLS gives transparent, explainable slopes ($m$) that factory managers can directly understand.''
\end{qabox}

\begin{qabox}[Q2: What do the slope ($m$) and intercept ($c$) represent physically in your formula $y = mx + c$?]
\textbf{Answer:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
    \item \textbf{Slope ($m$):} Represents the \textit{mechanical recovery efficiency} of that oil seed (e.g., $m = 0.422$ means 42.2\% of the groundnut seed mass is extracted as liquid oil).
    \item \textbf{Intercept ($c$):} Represents the \textit{machine tare absorption / residual loss} (typically $-2$ to $-3$ Liters). This is the initial oil absorbed by the expeller screw flights, barrel cage, and filter cloth before clean oil discharges into the collection tank.
\end{itemize}
\end{qabox}

\begin{qabox}[Q3: What are your model's evaluation metrics, and what do they prove?]
\textbf{Answer:} ``We evaluated using Scikit-Learn's $R^2$ Score and Mean Absolute Error (MAE):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
    \item $\mathbf{R^2 > 0.999}$: Proves that over 99.9\% of the variation in extracted oil is directly explained by the input seed weight.
    \item $\mathbf{\text{MAE} \le 2.4\text{ Liters}}$: On a large 2,000 kg batch yielding ~845 Liters, our average error is only $\pm 2.4\text{ L}$, which represents an error rate under 0.3\%.''
\end{itemize}
\end{qabox}

\begin{qabox}[Q4: How does the system detect `production inefficiency' as claimed in Slide 4?]
\textbf{Answer:} ``When an operator logs an actual batch (e.g., 2,000 kg Groundnut yielding 750 Liters instead of the ML predicted 845 Liters), the system computes extraction variance: $\Delta = \text{Actual} - \text{Predicted}$. If $\Delta < -5\%$, it triggers an inefficiency alert, indicating issues such as worn-out screw worms, pressing temperature dropping below 45$^\circ$C, or seeds having excess moisture.''
\end{qabox}

\begin{qabox}[Q5: Where did you get your dataset? Is it real or synthetic?]
\textbf{Answer:} ``Our Primary ML Dataset was structured from empirical batch extraction data aligned with published recovery ratios from \textbf{ICAR} (Indian Council of Agricultural Research) and \textbf{TNAU} (Tamil Nadu Agricultural University). The operational ERP dataset was seeded programmatically in MongoDB to simulate a live 68-batch factory environment.''
\end{qabox}

\begin{qabox}[Q6: How does the Node.js backend communicate with the Python ML model?]
\textbf{Answer:} ``The Express backend spawns \texttt{ml/train\_model.py} as a child process using Node's \texttt{child\_process.execFile} with CLI arguments (\texttt{--oil} and \texttt{--seeds}) and receives structured JSON. Furthermore, we designed a \textbf{dual-engine fallback}: if Python is unavailable on a client machine, the backend seamlessly switches to an internal JavaScript statistical regression engine (\texttt{simple-statistics}), ensuring uninterrupted uptime.''
\end{qabox}

\begin{qabox}[Q7: Why did you train separate models for each oil variety instead of one single model?]
\textbf{Answer:} ``Different oil seeds have fundamentally different biological oil contents (e.g., Coconut has ~58\% oil, while Mustard has only ~34\%). Training separate models per oil type produces direct, dedicated slope coefficients ($m$) that physically match each seed's recovery rate, avoiding categorical distortion.''
\end{qabox}

\begin{qabox}[Q8: What range of seed input batches does your model support?]
\textbf{Answer:} ``Our model supports commercial factory batches ranging from \textbf{500 kg to 5,000 kg}, which covers typical industrial daily production runs in small to medium-scale oil mills.''
\end{qabox}

\begin{qabox}[Q9: How do you prevent overselling or race conditions during customer orders?]
\textbf{Answer:} ``Through atomic database operations in MongoDB. The sales controller validates that $\text{currentLiters} \ge \text{requestedQuantity}$ before committing. If valid, it atomically decrements the tank and immediately emits a Socket.io event to synchronize all connected screens.''
\end{qabox}

\begin{qabox}[Q10: What is the main innovation of your project compared to standard ERP software?]
\textbf{Answer:} ``Traditional ERPs only record past transactions. Our system is proactive: it integrates \textbf{Machine Learning prediction directly into the batch creation flow}, providing real-time extraction efficiency benchmarks and automated tank telemetry via WebSockets.''
\end{qabox}
